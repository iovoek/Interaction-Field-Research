We're Measuring the Wrong Layer of Reality

Surface variables, deep structure, genuine transactions, and the Interaction Field: the conceptual foundation for understanding why complex systems fail.

By Glen Brackmann with AI research assistance  |  2026  |  Exchange Is the Equation

Every complex system has two layers. The first is the observable surface: prices, symptoms, clicks, test results, comparable sales, engagement metrics. These are the things we can measure easily, the things that show up in dashboards and reports and headlines. The second is the invariant deep structure: the underlying pattern of genuine interactions that generates the surface.

Human beings have spent centuries analyzing complex systems by starting at the surface and trying to infer the deep structure. This is the wrong direction. It is not merely suboptimal. It is mathematically guaranteed to compound error as system complexity increases.

This article introduces the four core concepts of the Interaction Field Theory: surface variables, deep structure, genuine transactions, and the Interaction Field itself. These are the building blocks for everything that follows in this series.

Surface Variables vs. Deep Structure

A surface variable is any observable output of a complex system. It is what you can measure without understanding the system. It is the thing that shows up first, that is easiest to track, and that most analysis begins with.

Deep structure is the invariant pattern of interactions that generates the surface. It is not directly observable. It has to be inferred, or better, it has to be the starting point from which the surface is predicted.

The distinction is not philosophical. It is mathematical. Surface variables are variable: they change with every observation, every transaction, every event. Deep structure is invariant: it changes slowly, if at all, and it is the thing that determines the range within which surface variables can move.

The Backwards Problem occurs when analysis starts at the surface variable and tries to infer the deep structure. The correct approach, which we call structure-first analysis, starts at the deep structure and uses it to predict, interpret, and contextualize the surface.

What Is a Genuine Transaction?

Genuine Transaction

A genuine transaction is a completed, verified exchange between two parties that constitutes real value formation. It is not a listed price, a bid, an offer, a sentiment, or a prediction. It is the actual exchange itself. In trading cards: a verified completed sale on TCGPlayer or eBay. In stocks: an exchange-reported trade. In education: a measurable intellectual exchange where knowledge actually transfers. In supply chains: an auditable information-sharing event. In social media: a reciprocal communication where both parties respond and engage.

The distinction between a genuine transaction and a surface signal is the distinction between the thing that actually forms value and the thing that merely reflects it. A listed price on eBay is not a genuine transaction. It is a surface signal. The completed sale is the genuine transaction. The listed price may or may not reflect reality. The completed sale is reality.

This matters because genuine transactions are the only thing that reduces uncertainty about value. A listing tells you what someone hopes to receive. A completed sale tells you what someone actually paid. The first is noise. The second is signal. And the more genuine transactions you have, the lower your uncertainty about the true value of the thing being exchanged.

The Interaction Field

Interaction Field

The Interaction Field is the complete set of genuine transactions that have occurred within a market or system, weighted by recency and relevance. It is the deep structure from which surface variables emerge. It is not a metaphor. It is a mathematical object: a function that maps transaction counts to value uncertainty, following a logistic curve with a characteristic inflection point n_c.

The Interaction Field Equation states that the value V of an asset is a function of the number of genuine transactions n in its field:

V(n) = V_max / (1 + e^(-k(n - n_c)))

This is a logistic function. It has three important properties. First, it starts near zero for very small n (few transactions, high uncertainty). Second, it grows rapidly through an inflection point at n_c (the zone of maximum information gain per transaction). Third, it asymptotes toward V_max for large n (the market has converged on a stable value).

The practical implication is that assets near their inflection point n_c have the highest information density per transaction. This is where the largest analytical edge exists for structure-first analysts. This is also where the Backwards Problem is most dangerous: surface variables near the inflection point are most volatile and most misleading.

The Aha Moment

The insight that ties all of this together is simple enough to state in one sentence: the exchange is not evidence of the value; the exchange is the equation.

In every domain where value is formed through genuine exchange, the interaction pattern is not a proxy for value. It is the mechanism by which value comes to exist. Listed prices, engagement metrics, test scores, and symptoms are all downstream outputs of the interaction field. They are effects, not causes. Analyzing them as if they were causes is the Backwards Problem.

This is why pharmaceutical companies fail 90% of the time in clinical trials: they start from a molecular target (a surface variable) and try to infer biological system behavior (the deep structure). It is why social media platforms are destroying their own revenue: they optimize for engagement (a surface variable) instead of genuine connection (the deep structure). It is why real estate appraisals are systematically wrong in thin markets: they rely on comparable sales (surface variables) when the transaction count is too low to have formed a stable Interaction Field.
