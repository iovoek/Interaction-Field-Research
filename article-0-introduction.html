Exchange Is the Equation

Article 0 · Introduction

What This Is

An ongoing, publicly visible research project exploring a single structural insight: that most complex systems are being analyzed from the wrong direction, and that the pieces needed to correct this are already scattered across dozens of fields, waiting to be connected.

By Glen Brackmann with AI research assistance  |  2026  |  Exchange Is the Equation

This is a passion project. It is not a peer-reviewed journal. It is not backed by an institution. I am not a research scientist. I am someone who noticed a pattern, followed it further than expected, and decided to build it in public rather than in private.

The work here is ongoing. It gets revised, extended, and sometimes corrected in real time. When we find errors, we fix them and document the fix. When we find limitations, we say so. The site is a living document.

What follows is an explanation of the project: what it claims, where it came from, and what to expect from the series.

The Observation

In most complex systems that human beings try to understand and optimize, we start from what is easy to measure and try to work backward toward what actually matters. Stock prices instead of the trading activity that constitutes them. Engagement metrics instead of the quality of human connection they are supposed to represent. Molecular targets instead of the phenotypic behavior of living cells. Zoning maps instead of the patterns of human movement that make neighborhoods alive. Standardized test scores instead of the learning processes they are supposed to reflect.

This seems intuitive. Measure what you can see, then infer what you cannot. But in complex systems, this approach does not merely underperform. It actively degrades the thing it is trying to measure. The errors compound. And the more complex the system, the worse it gets.

The alternative is to start from the deep structure, the actual pattern of genuine interactions within a system, and let the surface metrics be an output rather than an input. This is not a new idea in any single field. Complexity economists have been saying it for decades. So have systems biologists, urban theorists, and market microstructure researchers. What none of them have done, as far as we can find, is connect these observations into a single formal framework and show that the same mathematical structure governs all of them.

That is what this project attempts.

The pieces are already here. They are scattered across trading theory, drug discovery, information theory, urban planning, ecology, AI architecture, and half a dozen other fields. The only thing missing is the connection between them. That connection is what we are building.

Why Connections, Not Data Points

There is a reason this framework is called the Interaction Field and not the "Data Field" or the "Measurement Framework." The central claim is that value, meaning, and structural health in complex systems emerge not from individual components but from the connective tissue between them.

This is visible everywhere once you start looking for it. In trading card markets, value is not stored in any single card. It is constituted by the density and velocity of genuine transactions flowing through the market. In neural networks, intelligence does not reside in any single node. It emerges from the pattern of weighted connections between nodes. In cities, vitality does not come from any single building. It comes from the density and diversity of human interactions that the built environment enables or prevents.

In modern AI systems, the most powerful architectures are not the ones with the best individual models. They are the ones that connect multiple models, multiple data sources, and multiple reasoning processes together and study the flow between them rather than the static content of any single source. The creativity, the novel outputs, the things that did not exist before, emerge from the interaction between systems, not from any one system alone.

This insight is self-referential. This very research project is a product of interaction fields. When a human and an AI collaborate on analysis, the output is not the human's ideas plus the AI's processing. It is not additive. Something genuinely new emerges from the interaction itself, something that neither party would have produced alone and that cannot be cleanly attributed to either one. The interaction is generative. The connective tissue between different modes of thinking produces things that did not previously exist in either mode.

This is not mysticism. It is observable and measurable. The mathematical framework in Article 5 formalizes exactly how and why interaction density produces compounding returns that isolated optimization cannot replicate. The social media simulation in Article 3 demonstrates it with empirically anchored numbers. The trading card analysis in Article 1 shows it in the simplest possible domain. But the principle is the same everywhere: the field of interactions is where value lives, not in the individual points that the field connects.

The Orientation Conjecture

We call the formal version of this the Orientation Conjecture: that in any complex system, starting your analysis from the deep structure (the interaction field) and letting surface metrics be outputs will always outperform starting from surface metrics and trying to infer deep structure, and that the performance gap grows with system complexity.

The mathematical proof is complete under its stated assumptions. The empirical question of whether those assumptions hold universally remains open. That is why it is called a conjecture and not a law. We are honest about the difference between what the math proves and what the real world confirms.

What we can say is that across every domain we have tested so far, the pattern holds. Trading cards, social media platforms, pharmaceutical drug discovery, semiconductor manufacturing, urban development, real estate markets, education systems, supply chain logistics, venture capital allocation. In each case, the systems that orient from deep structure outward outperform the systems that orient from surface metrics inward. And in each case, the systems that orient backwards do not merely underperform. They actively degrade their own foundations over time.

We call that degradation pattern the Backwards Problem.

How This Started

The project has multiple origin points, not a single moment of discovery.

The first was a text message. I was building a trading card analysis system and my father suggested it could be applied to other markets. In explaining why trading cards are structurally unique, I articulated something I had not previously put into words: that the volume of genuine transactions does not merely correlate with value, it constitutes value. That observation, once formalized, turned out to have implications far beyond trading cards.

The second was a YouTube video. Dami Lee, an architect and creator who runs the channel DamiLee, published a video in January 2026 called "This New Pyramid Theory Explains the Missing Evidence". In it, she presents Huni Choi's "unbuilding theory" of pyramid construction, which proposes that the pyramids were carved from the top down rather than built from the bottom up. The structural logic of that proposal became the central metaphor for this project: the idea that you can look at a finished structure and completely misread the direction in which it was made.

The third was an instinct about how modern technology actually works. Working with AI systems, connecting multiple data sources, building pipelines that link different tools together, it became clear that the interesting outputs never come from any single input or any single model. They come from the connections. They come from studying how data flows between systems, not from studying the data points in isolation. The interaction between fields is where new things get created. That observation kept appearing in every domain I looked at, and eventually it became impossible to ignore the pattern.

None of these origin points alone would have produced this project. Together, interacting with each other, they produced something none of them contained individually. Which is, in a way, the theory demonstrating itself.

What This Does Not Claim

This is not a grand unified theory of everything. It is a specific analytical reorientation, a switch in the direction from which you approach complex problems, that appears to produce better results across a wide range of domains. The pieces were already here. Researchers across many fields have been saying versions of this for decades. What we are doing is connecting those observations formally and testing whether the connection holds.

We do not claim originality for the underlying ideas. W. Brian Arthur's complexity economics. Albert Kyle's market microstructure theory. David Swinney's phenotypic drug discovery research. Jane Jacobs' urban theory. Smitha Milli's work on algorithmic amplification. The building blocks belong to the researchers who came before us. What we claim is original is the synthesis: the formal connection between orientation direction and error accumulation, and the application of that single framework across multiple independent domains.

We do not claim that everything on this site is correct. We claim that it is our best current attempt, built with the best data we can find, and subject to revision. The simulation parameters are always provisional. When better empirical data becomes available, the parameters change, the simulation is rerun, and the results are updated. That is how research is supposed to work.

How We Work

The research is conducted with AI assistance. The AI helps with literature review, mathematical verification, simulation coding, and fact-checking. The conceptual direction, the creative leaps, and the editorial judgment are human. This collaboration is itself an expression of the theory: the output of the interaction is not reducible to either party's contribution alone.

We backlink to all data sources. We run simulations with documented seed values so they can be reproduced. We publish the simulation source code. We state our assumptions explicitly so they can be challenged. We include a full article on how this theory could be wrong because a theory that cannot be falsified is not a theory.

The site is a living document. When we find errors, we correct them publicly. When we find new domains where the framework applies, we add them. When we discover limitations, we document them. The work is never finished.

The articles are designed to be read in order, but each one can stand alone:

  • Article 1: The Backwards Problem
    How the theory emerged from a trading card insight, and why the direction of analysis matters more than the quality of data.
  • Article 2: We're Measuring the Wrong Layer of Reality
    The formal distinction between surface variables and deep structure, and why optimizing the wrong layer guarantees failure.
  • Article 3: Burning the House Down for Kindling
    A fully empirically anchored simulation showing how optimizing for engagement destroys long-term platform economics.
  • Article 4: Value Is Not a Number

    The formal mathematics. The Orientation Theorem, the proof, and the Monte Carlo validation.
  • Article 6: The Same Mistake, Everywhere
    Nine independent domains where the same structural pattern operates.
  • Article 7: How This Theory Could Be Wrong
    The conditions under which the framework fails, the predictions that would falsify it, and the honest limitations.
  • Article 8: What Comes Next
    The research roadmap. What domains are next, what experiments would test the predictions, and where collaboration is needed.
  • Article 9: The Drug Industry's 90% Problem

More articles will follow as the research expands. The framework applies to more domains than we have written about so far, and each new application tests whether the pattern holds or breaks.

Where This Goes

The project will continue to expand into new domains. It will also continue to deepen the domains already covered. The existing articles will be updated as better data becomes available. The framework itself may change as we discover its limitations.

If you are new here, start with Article 1. If you want the formal mathematics, go to Article 5. If you want to see how the theory could be wrong, that is Article 7. If you want the most developed applications, read Article 3 or Article 9.

Thank you for reading. The work continues.
