Social Media & Algorithmic Amplification

Milli et al., 2025
Engagement, user satisfaction, and the amplification of divisive content on social media
Smitha Milli, Micah Carroll, Yike Wang, Sashrika Pandey, Sebastian Zhao, Anca D. Dragan
PNAS Nexus, Vol. 4, Issue 3, March 2025. doi:10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf062. Cited 238 times.
A preregistered algorithmic audit of Twitter's engagement-based ranking algorithm. Key finding: relative to a reverse-chronological baseline, Twitter's algorithm amplifies emotionally charged, out-group hostile content that users say makes them feel worse about their political out-group. Critically, users do not prefer the political tweets selected by the algorithm, demonstrating a measurable divergence between revealed preferences (engagement) and stated preferences (what users actually want). This is the most direct empirical support for the central claim of Article 3: that social media platforms are optimizing for the wrong variable.
PMC Full Text PNAS Nexus Code (GitHub) Data (Harvard Dataverse)
Germano, Gómez & Sobbrio, 2026
Ranking for engagement: How social media algorithms fuel misinformation and polarization
Fabrizio Germano, Vicenç Gómez, Francesco Sobbrio
Journal of Public Economics, 2026. doi:10.1016/j.jpubeco.2026.105589
Develops a theoretical model of the dynamic feedback loop between recommendation algorithms and user behavior. The model uncovers a structural trade-off: increasing the weight on social interactions (likes, shares) increases user engagement but also misinformation and polarization. Empirical evidence from Italy and the United States shows that Facebook's 2018 "Meaningful Social Interactions" update contributed to increased ideological extremism and affective polarization. This provides direct empirical evidence for the error feedback loop described in Article 3.
SSRN Preprint Journal DOI
Pew Research Center, 2025
Americans' Social Media Use 2025
Pew Research Center
Pew Research Center Internet & Technology, November 2025. Survey of 5,022 U.S. adults.
Comprehensive survey data on social media platform usage, user satisfaction, and attitudes toward social media. Provides baseline data on platform adoption rates and user sentiment that contextualizes the algorithmic effects described in Milli et al. and Germano et al.
Pew Fact Sheet Full Report

Pharmaceutical R&D & Clinical Trial Failure

Sun et al., 2022
Why 90% of clinical drug development fails and how to improve it
Duxin Sun, Wei Gao, Hongxiang Hu, Simon Zhou
Acta Pharmaceutica Sinica B, Vol. 12, Issue 7, July 2022, pp. 3049-3062.
Comprehensive analysis of the structural causes of the 90% clinical trial failure rate. Identifies poor target selection and inadequate disease model translation as primary drivers. Supports the Interaction Field framework's diagnosis that target-based drug discovery is oriented from the surface inward, producing compounding error across development phases.
PMC Full Text
BIO / Informa, 2021
Clinical Development Success Rates and Contributing Factors 2011–2020
Biotechnology Innovation Organization (BIO) / Informa Pharma Intelligence / QLS Advisors
BIO Industry Analysis, 2021. Analysis of 13,000+ clinical and regulatory transitions across 1,779 companies.
The definitive industry dataset on clinical trial success rates. Key statistics: overall likelihood of approval from Phase I = 7.9% (not the commonly cited 10%). Phase II is the single biggest bottleneck with only 28.9% success rate. Phase III success rate = 57.8%. NDA/BLA to approval = 90.6%. These numbers are the empirical foundation for the pharmaceutical section of this research.
BIO Report Page Full PDF
Sadri, 2023
Is Target-Based Drug Discovery Efficient? Discovery and Off-Target Effects
Amir Sadri
Journal of Medicinal Chemistry, 2023. doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.2c01737
Systematic analysis of whether target-based drug discovery is an efficient approach. Finds fundamental efficiency problems that are not solvable by better target selection, because the problem is the direction of analysis, not the quality of individual targets. Directly supports the Backwards Problem diagnosis applied to pharmaceutical R&D.
ACS Publication
Vincent et al., 2022
Phenotypic Drug Discovery: Recent successes, lessons learned and new directions
Fabien Vincent et al.
Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, 2022. PMC9708951.
Comprehensive review of phenotypic screening approaches in drug discovery. Finds that phenotypic approaches have higher clinical success rates than target-based approaches across multiple disease areas. Provides the empirical basis for the claim that starting from the deep structure (complex biological function) produces better outcomes than starting from the surface (isolated molecular targets).
PMC Full Text

Complexity Economics & Increasing Returns

Arthur, 1994
Increasing Returns and Path Dependence in the Economy
W. Brian Arthur
University of Michigan Press, 1994.
Foundational work establishing that economic systems exhibit increasing returns and path dependence, meaning that small early advantages can lock in outcomes that are not necessarily optimal. This is the economic complexity framework that the Interaction Field Theory extends and formalizes. Arthur's work establishes that the surface of economic outcomes (prices, market shares) is generated by deep structural dynamics (increasing returns, network effects) that cannot be inferred from surface analysis alone.
SFI Profile Google Scholar YouTube Talk

Systems Medicine & Precision Biology

Hood & Price, 2014
Demystifying Disease, Democratizing Health Care
Leroy Hood, Nathan Price
Science Translational Medicine, 2014.
Articulates the P4 medicine framework (predictive, preventive, personalized, participatory) as an alternative to the current disease-centric model. Argues that medicine must shift from analyzing disease at the level of symptoms and isolated biomarkers (surface) to analyzing the whole-person biological network (deep structure). This is the systems medicine equivalent of the Orientation Conjecture.
ISB Profile Google Scholar
Robinson et al., 2008–present
The Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO)
Peter N. Robinson et al.
Ongoing project. HPO website: human-phenotype-ontology.org
A standardized vocabulary for phenotypic abnormalities in human disease, enabling deep phenotyping for precision medicine. The HPO represents an attempt to characterize the deep structure of disease presentation rather than relying on surface-level diagnostic categories. Robinson's work is a direct application of structure-first analysis in clinical medicine.
HPO Website Google Scholar Twitter/X

Pyramid Construction & Archaeological Research

Huni Choi, 2025–2026
Top-Down Pyramid Construction Theory
Huni Choi (independent researcher)
YouTube / Instagram (@hunichoipyramid). Covered by Open Culture, February 2026.
Proposes that the Egyptian pyramids were constructed from the top down rather than the bottom up, based on analysis of the keying stone evidence and construction logistics. This theory is the origin of the Backwards Problem concept in this research: the realization that the direction of construction, and by analogy the direction of analysis, fundamentally changes the error accumulation properties of the process.
