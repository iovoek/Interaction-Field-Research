# Social Media Algorithm Simulation Results

## Summary

This simulation compares two algorithmic strategies over 36 months with a 100,000-user cohort:

1. **Engagement-Based (Current):** Optimizes for clicks, reactions, shares. Promotes emotionally charged content because it generates more surface-level interaction.
2. **Connection-Based (Proposed):** Optimizes for reciprocal interaction, trust formation, and conversation depth. Applies the Interaction Field framework by orienting toward deep structure rather than surface metrics.

## Key Findings

| Metric | Month 1 (Engagement) | Month 1 (Connection) | Month 36 (Engagement) | Month 36 (Connection) |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Total Monthly Revenue | $3.45M | $1.62M | $1.01M | $2.66M |
| User Retention | 100% | 100% | 54.9% | 87.4% |
| User Satisfaction | 0.65 | 0.65 | 0.25 | 0.87 |
| Data Quality | 0.55 | 0.55 | 0.24 | 0.92 |
| Effective CPM | $7.56 | $7.56 | $4.56 | $10.82 |

## The Business Case

- **Revenue crossover point: Month 18** -- the connection-based algorithm surpasses engagement-based in monthly revenue
- **Short-term cost (Months 1-17):** $17.1M less revenue during transition
- **Long-term gain (Months 18-36):** $17.5M more revenue after crossover
- **Cumulative 36-month revenue:** Nearly identical ($75.5M vs $75.8M), with connection-based accelerating
- **Projected 48-month gap:** Connection-based would be significantly ahead due to compounding retention and CPM advantages

## Why This Happens (The Backwards Problem)

The engagement-based algorithm optimizes for a **surface metric** (clicks) that is only weakly correlated with the **deep structure** (genuine user value). This creates a compounding error:

1. Algorithm promotes outrage content (high clicks)
2. User satisfaction erodes (content does not serve genuine needs)
3. Users leave (retention drops from 100% to 55%)
4. Remaining users produce lower-quality behavioral data (they are reactive, not intentional)
5. Lower data quality means worse ad targeting
6. Worse targeting means lower CPM ($7.56 drops to $4.56)
7. Lower CPM * fewer users = revenue collapse

The connection-based algorithm avoids this cascade by optimizing for the deep structure directly.

## The Argument to Platforms

You do not need negativity to make money. You are currently:
- Losing 45% of your users over 3 years
- Watching your CPM decline by 40%
- Degrading the quality of the very behavioral data your business model depends on
- Creating brand safety problems that drive away premium advertisers

The engagement-based approach is not even good capitalism. It is the Backwards Problem applied to your own revenue model.
